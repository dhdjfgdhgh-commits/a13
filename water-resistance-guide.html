Water Resistance Guide

Water ratings are among the most misunderstood numbers on a watch. Here is what they mean, and how to keep the inside of your watch dry.

1.0 Why the number misleads

A rating such as “50 m” describes the pressure a watch passed in a static lab test. Moving water, temperature changes and pressing buttons underwater all add stress the test does not include. That is why a 50 m watch is not for swimming.

3.0 Crowns and seals

Rubber gaskets seal the case back, crystal and crown. A screw-down crown presses its gasket closed and is the mark of a watch built for the water. Always push in or screw down the crown before the watch gets wet.

4.0 What wears seals out

  • Hot showers, saunas and hot tubs (heat makes gaskets expand and contract)
  • Sun cream, soap and salt water left to dry
  • Time: gaskets age even when the watch is unused
  • Opening the case back for a battery change

5.0 Keep it tested

If you swim with your watch, have the water resistance tested every year or two and after any battery change or repair. Rinse in fresh water after the sea or a pool. If you see mist under the crystal, stop wearing it and have it checked quickly.
