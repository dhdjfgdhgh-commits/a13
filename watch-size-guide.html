Watch Size Guide

Case diameter is only one number. Lug-to-lug length, thickness and strap width decide whether a watch actually sits well on your wrist.

1.0 Measure your wrist

Wrap a strip of paper around the wrist where the watch sits, just above the wrist bone. Mark where it overlaps and measure it flat. Note whether the top of your wrist is flat or rounded; flat wrists can wear slightly larger cases.

2.0 Case diameter

WristTypical comfortable diameter
14–15.5 cm34–38 mm
15.5–17 cm36–40 mm
17–18.5 cm38–42 mm
18.5–20 cm40–44 mm
20 cm and up42–46 mm

Starting points, not rules. Style and personal taste matter.

3.0 Lug-to-lug matters most

Lug-to-lug is the length from the tip of the top lugs to the tip of the bottom lugs. If it is longer than the flat top of your wrist, the lugs overhang and the watch looks too big, whatever its diameter.

4.0 Thickness

Under about 10 mm slips under a shirt cuff. Sports and dive watches are often 12–15 mm. A thick case on a small wrist sits high and can feel top-heavy.

5.0 Strap width

A common rule is that strap width is roughly half the case diameter: about 18 mm for 36 mm, 20 mm for 40 mm and 22 mm for 44 mm. A strap that tapers towards the buckle feels lighter.
